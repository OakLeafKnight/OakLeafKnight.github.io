Principles

What we are going for

Four rules borrowed from the disciplines we respect — Danfoss's single-red restraint and Apple's deference — for a team that competes hard.

01

One red, earned

Red is the team. Use it for what matters and let white carry the layout, so red never goes quiet from overuse.

02

Precision under speed

Aggressive display type, disciplined system around it. The tension is the brand.

03

Say it once, clearly

Hierarchy over decoration. One headline, one focus, one action per surface.

04

Built to be built on

Everything is a token, not a one-off — the same red, type, and chart colors resolve identically in a post, a shirt, and a report.

Logo

The logo

The team's signature. Always use the official files (team drive or a mentor) — never rebuild it yourself.

Red on white. White on red or dark. Those are your options.

✓ Do
  • Use the official files, full and unchanged
  • Give it a "bubble" of clear space (about the height of the round b badge)
  • Use the white version on red or dark backgrounds
  • Make it big enough to see clearly
✕ Don't
  • Stretch, squish, tilt, or rotate it
  • Change its color or add glow / shadow / outline
  • Retype the words yourself in a different font
  • Put it on a busy photo where you can't see it

Quick tip: don't put red text on a dark background — it's hard to read. Red belongs on white, white belongs on red. That flip is the heart of our look.
The red budget — one red per thing. Put red on the single most important element (the score, the button, or one accent) and make everything else carbon, gray, or white. If two things are red, neither wins. This one rule keeps the whole look disciplined.

Full spec — HEX #C82027 · RGB 200 32 39 · CMYK 0 84 80 22 · Pantone 186 C (nearest). Contrast: red on white is 5.7:1 (great for headlines and buttons; use Carbon for long paragraphs).

Device 01 · the container

Rounded

Soft rounded corners are the shell for everything — cards, buttons, tags, banners, inputs, media. Easy to reproduce in any tool, and the friendly base the sharper accents play against.

One radius per element; never mix a sharp corner with a rounded one.

Radius scale

ElementRadius
Tags / chips / pillsfull pill (½ height)
Buttons / inputs10px
Cards / callouts14px
Banners / hero panels16–20px
Images / media12px

Filled (red or carbon, no border) or white with a 1.5px N300 hairline. That's the whole container system.

Device 02 · focus accent

The Bracket

Four open corner ticks that frame the one focal thing — the hero stat, the key photo, a featured callout. They never close into a box; the open corners are the whole idea.

Frames a region, not every element. One bracketed thing per surface.
PropertyRule
Tick length12–16% of the framed area's short side (min 12px, max ~28px)
Stroke3–4px, scaling with size
ColorRed on light, white on dark; sits on or just inside the corners
NeverClose it into a box, bracket tiny inline tags, or add a heavy border too
Bracket vs Trace: the Trace leads the eye to a headline with a line; the Bracket frames a region with ticks. Use one per element — never both on the same thing.
Device 03 · engineering scope

The Pocket

A soft, filleted triangle drawn from the lightening pockets machined into our robot. It's an engineering signal — when it appears, the content is about the machine. Keep it out of general brand, sponsor, and hype material.

Solid — focus/marker
Outline — container
Nested — subsystem callout

✓ Use it for

  • Robot reveal graphics & posts
  • CAD / design breakdowns
  • Technical docs, subsystem callouts
  • Build-season "in the shop" content

✕ Not for

  • General team / community posts
  • Sponsor thank-yous
  • Match-result hype
  • Anywhere the general system should speak
TRUSS PATTERN — low-opacity background for technical layouts
Photo mask — frame robot/CAD imagery inside a pocket
Keep vertices soft (hold the fillet ratio at every size), one orientation per composition, and reserve it for engineering content.
Device 04 · leading line

The Trace

A line enters on the diagonal, bends once to flat, and lands in a small rectangular pad — pointing the eye straight at the headline. The diagonal entry is what makes the eye travel, so it's always angled, never a square bracket.

The five moves — no dots, no circles. It always resolves in a rectangular pad.

Spec

PropertyRule
EntryAlways 45° from the nearest corner/edge — the angle is what leads the eye
BendExactly one, mitered to flat. No second bend, no 90° bracket
TerminalRectangular pad (≈2.5× stroke wide × 1.5× tall, r2) or runs off-frame. Never round
Strokesocial/hero 6–8px · slide 5–6px · doc accent 2–3px
Flat run≥ 1.5× the width of the thing it leads to; pad sits just past the headline
ColorRed on light, white on dark; neutral gray for faint background fields

In Word, PowerPoint & Instagram

Same three moves everywhere: from a corner at 45° → bend to flat → end in a rectangle under the headline.
✓ Do
  • Always enter on the diagonal — it's a leading line
  • One bend only, landing under the focal element
  • Rectangular pad, or run it off-frame
  • Point it at the headline / score
✕ Don't
  • Use a square 90° bracket or frame
  • Zigzag with multiple bends
  • Use round terminals or dots
  • Float it with nothing to lead to

Captions & hashtags

What to write

The image grabs attention; the caption gives it meaning. Short, upbeat, specific.

Our voice

  • Proud, not bragging — celebrate the team.
  • Positive always — even a tough match is "we learned a ton."
  • Specific beats vague.
  • Thank people — sponsors, volunteers, mentors.

Starter lines

  • "Final: Breakaway 86, [Opponent] 58. 🔴"
  • "Match day! Watch us at [event] this weekend."
  • "Huge thanks to [sponsor] for making this season possible."
  • "Behind the scenes in the pit today 🔧"
Always use our hashtags: #Breakaway #FRC3937 #omgrobots #FIRSTrobotics, plus event tags when you have them.

Before posting people: only post students who've okayed it; never share full names of minors, addresses, or exact schedules. When unsure, ask a mentor.

Using Canva

Making stuff in Canva

Canva is free and the easiest place for posts, flyers, and slides. Do steps 1–3 once and it's saved.

  1. Start the right size. Create a design → Custom size → 1080 × 1080 (square) or 1080 × 1350 (tall). Story is 1080 × 1920.
  2. Add our colors. Click the rainbow + and paste C82027, 181416, FFFFFF.
  3. Set the fonts. Search Chakra Petch for big words, Roboto for sentences.
  4. Upload the logo. Uploads → Upload files → drag it in. White logo on red/dark, red logo on white.
  5. Keep it simple. One message, lots of empty space, logo in a corner with room.
  6. Export. Share → Download → PNG.
Even faster: the Post builder above makes finished posts without Canva. Use Canva for custom flyers or multi-photo layouts.

Same colors, fonts, and logo rules everywhere. A few pointers:

Feed posts

  • One clear message; roomy layout.
  • Score leads on match posts (Chakra Petch).
  • Logo small in a corner with clear space.
  • Skip heavy filters — they mess with our red.

Stories & reels

  • 1080 × 1920; keep a 10% edge margin clear for UI.
  • Big type, few words.
  • The Trace leads the eye in a reel intro or lower-third.

Questions

FAQ

Tap a question to open it.

I've never designed anything. Where do I start?

Go to the Post builder, pick "Match win," change the score, and download. You just made an on-brand post.

What are the exact colors?

Red #C82027, Carbon #181416, White #FFFFFF, plus Ember #8E1519 for small accents. Tap any swatch in Color to copy.

Which font goes where?

Chakra Petch for short punchy phrases and numbers; Roboto for full sentences.

Where do I get the logo files?

From the team drive or a mentor. Always use the real files — don't screenshot or rebuild it.

Can I change the logo's color or stretch it?

No. Red on light, white on red/dark, always full proportions, no effects.

What are those triangle / line shapes?

Brand devices. The Pocket (soft triangle) is for engineering/robot content only; the Trace is a leading line that points at your headline. Everyday posts mostly just need color, type, rounded shapes, and the logo.

The download didn't work on my phone.

Just screenshot the preview — the square preview is already the right shape.

Can I post pictures of other students?

Only if they're okay with it. Never share full names of minors, addresses, or exact schedules. Ask a mentor if unsure.

Who do I ask for help?

A mentor or the media/controls lead. It's always fine to check before posting.
