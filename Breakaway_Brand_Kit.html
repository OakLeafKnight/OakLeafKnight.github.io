Quick tip: Don't put red text on a dark/black background — it's hard to read. Red belongs on white, and white belongs on red. That flip is the heart of our look.

Printing something? Tell the print shop our red is Pantone 186 C (or CMYK 0 / 84 / 80 / 22). They'll know what that means.

Fonts

Our two fonts

Two fonts, two jobs. Using the right one for the right thing is 90% of looking on-brand.

For big, bold words → Chakra Petch
BREAKAWAY 3937
Headlines, scores, team number, big statements. It has that sharp, techy, cut-corner look that matches our logo. Use the Bold weight for maximum punch.
For normal sentences → Roboto
The quick brown fox jumps.
Captions, paragraphs, details, dates — anything that's a full sentence. It's clean and easy to read at any size.
Both fonts are free. Search “Chakra Petch” and “Roboto” on fonts.google.com and click Get font → Download. In Canva they're already built in — just type the names in the font box. This very page is already using them, so what you see is exactly right.

Can't get the fonts on your device? Use these free backups that come with most computers: Oswald or Bebas Neue in place of Chakra Petch, and Arial or Helvetica in place of Roboto. Not identical, but close enough in a pinch.

Heads up about the logo font: The word “BREAKAWAY” in our logo uses a special font (ARDestine) that we're only allowed to use inside the finished logo files. Don't download it to type your own text — use Chakra Petch for that instead. The logo is already made for you, so you never need that font anyway.

Logo

The logo

The logo is the team's signature. Always use the official files (ask a mentor or check the team drive) — never rebuild it yourself.

Red version on white. White version on red or dark. That's it — those are your options.

✓ Do this
  • Use the official logo files, full and un-changed
  • Give it a “bubble” of empty space so nothing crowds it
  • Use the white version on red or dark backgrounds
  • Make it big enough to see clearly
✕ Never do this
  • Stretch, squish, tilt, or rotate it
  • Change its color or add a glow/shadow/outline
  • Retype the words yourself in a different font
  • Put it on a busy photo where you can't see it
What's “clear space”? Imagine an invisible bubble around the logo about as tall as the round “b” badge. Keep text, edges, and other stuff out of that bubble. It keeps the logo looking clean and important.

Profile pictures: use just the round “b” badge — it stays sharp even when tiny. White badge on a red circle looks great as an Instagram avatar.

Captions & hashtags

What to actually write

The image grabs attention; the caption gives it meaning. Keep it short, upbeat, and specific.

Our voice

  • Proud, not bragging. Celebrate the team, not yourself.
  • Positive, always. Even a tough match is “we learned a ton.”
  • Specific beats vague. “Climbed in 11 of 12 matches” > “we did great.”
  • Thank people. Sponsors, volunteers, hosts, mentors.

Good starter lines

  • “Final: Breakaway 86, [Opponent] 58. 🔴”
  • “Match day! Watch us at [event] this weekend.”
  • “Huge thanks to [sponsor] for making this season possible.”
  • “Behind the scenes in the pit today 🔧”
Always use our hashtags so people can find us: #Breakaway #FRC3937 #omgrobots #FIRSTrobotics. Add a couple event-specific ones when you have them (like #RockCityRegional).

Before you tag or post about someone: only post students who've okayed it, and never share full names of minors, home addresses, or schedules that say exactly where someone will be. When unsure, ask a mentor first.

Using Canva

Making stuff in Canva (step by step)

Canva is free and the easiest place to make posts, flyers, and slides. Here's how to set it up so everything comes out on-brand. Do steps 1–3 once and it's saved for next time.

  1. Start the right size. Click Create a design → Custom size. For an Instagram post type 1080 × 1080 px (square) or 1080 × 1350 px (taller). For a story, 1080 × 1920 px.
  2. Add our colors. When you pick a color, click the rainbow + and paste our hex codes: C82027 (red), 181416 (carbon), FFFFFF (white). They'll stay in your recent colors.
  3. Set the fonts. In the text box, search Chakra Petch for big/bold words and Roboto for sentences.
  4. Upload the logo. Click Uploads → Upload files and add the official logo file. Drag it onto your design. Use the white logo on red/dark, the red logo on white.
  5. Keep it simple. One big message. Lots of empty space. Logo in a corner with room around it.
  6. Export. Click Share → Download → PNG. That file is ready to post.
Even faster: the builder above makes finished square/tall/story posts without Canva at all. Use Canva when you want something more custom, like a flyer or a multi-photo layout.
Everything else

Flyers, slides, and posters

Same three colors, same two fonts, same logo rules. A few extra pointers:

Slides (Google Slides / PowerPoint)

  • White background, Carbon text, red for titles/highlights.
  • Big Chakra Petch titles, Roboto body.
  • Logo small in a corner of each slide.
  • One idea per slide — don't cram.

Flyers & posters

  • Biggest thing = the one fact people must know (date, event).
  • Logo clearly visible, with clear space.
  • Leave a margin — don't run text to the very edge.
  • If printing, export as PDF and use the red = Pantone 186 C note.
Questions

FAQ — pretty much everything

Tap a question to open the answer.

I've never designed anything. Where do I actually start?

Go to Make an Instagram Post, pick “Match win,” change the numbers to your real score, and hit download. You just made an on-brand post. Everything else in this guide is for when you want to do more.

What are the exact colors again?

Red is #C82027, our near-black “Carbon” is #181416, and white is #FFFFFF. There's also a darker red called Ember (#8E1519) for small accents. Tap any swatch in the Colors section to copy the code.

Which font goes where?

Chakra Petch = big bold words (headlines, scores, team number). Roboto = normal sentences (captions, details). If you mix them up it'll look off, so when in doubt: is it a full sentence? Roboto. Is it a short punchy phrase? Chakra Petch.

Where do I get the logo files?

From the team's shared drive or a mentor — ask if you're not sure where. Always use the real files. Please don't screenshot the logo off a website or rebuild it yourself; it ends up blurry or wrong.

Can I change the logo's color to match my post?

No. The logo only comes in red (for light backgrounds) and white (for red/dark backgrounds). Changing colors, stretching, tilting, or adding effects is the fastest way to look off-brand.

What size should an Instagram post be?

1080 × 1080 (square) always works. 1080 × 1350 (taller) takes up more space in the feed and often does better. Stories are 1080 × 1920. The builder above makes all three.

Can I use filters or lots of emojis?

Skip heavy filters — they mess with our red. A tiny bit of brightness/contrast on a photo is fine. Emojis are okay in captions in small doses (a 🔴 or 💪), but keep them off the image itself.

Can I add my own colors, like blue or green?

For posts, stick to red, carbon, and white — that's what makes us recognizable. (There are extra colors for charts and graphs in the advanced section, but not for regular posts.)

Photo looks empty — should I fill the space?

Empty space is good! It's what makes designs look clean and professional. Resist the urge to fill every corner. One clear message with room around it beats a crowded post every time.

What do I write in the caption?

One or two upbeat sentences about what's happening, plus our hashtags. See Captions & hashtags for starter lines you can copy.

Which hashtags do we use?

#Breakaway #FRC3937 #omgrobots #FIRSTrobotics every time, plus event-specific ones when you have them. There's a copy button next to the hashtags in the builder.

Is it okay to post pictures of other students?

Only if they're okay with being posted. Never share full names of minors, home addresses, or a schedule showing exactly where someone will be. If you're unsure, ask a mentor before posting.

Can I post from my own account or does it have to be the team's?

Official team posts go on the team account, usually after a mentor gives the thumbs-up. You're always welcome to cheer us on from your own account using our hashtags.

The download button didn't work on my phone. Now what?

No problem — just screenshot the preview. On most phones the square preview is already the right shape. (Some phone browsers block auto-downloads; screenshots always work.)

Who do I ask if I'm still stuck?

Ask a mentor or the team's media/controls lead. It's always fine to check before posting — we'd rather answer a question than fix a post later.

Advanced · most people can skip this

For the media & data crew

Only need this if you're doing print files or making charts/graphs for the team. Everyone else is done — go make a post!

Print settings

Build print art in CMYK. Our red is Pantone 186 C / CMYK 0-84-80-22. Keep a 0.125" bleed and a 0.25" safe margin (keep important stuff inside that). Place logos as vector (.ai/.pdf/.svg) or at least 300 dpi. Logo minimum size: about 1 inch wide, or 24 px for the badge alone.

Chart & graph colors

For data visuals, red marks the important line/bar; everything else stays gray so red stands out. Full palette for charts:
